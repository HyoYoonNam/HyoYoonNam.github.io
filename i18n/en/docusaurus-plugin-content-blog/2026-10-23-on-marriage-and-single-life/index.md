> *A man with a wife and children has given hostages to fortune. In good undertakings and bad alike, they are a great hindrance to anyone who would do great things.* — [Francis Bacon, “Of Marriage and Single Life,” translated here from the Moonye edition](https://www.yes24.com/product/goods/147564050)

Quite a provocative thing to say. Would I really concentrate harder on my work if I had no children? Couldn’t the responsibility of having a child give me a reason to concentrate harder?

<!-- truncate -->

It’s exam season at university. I walk into the library determined to pull an all-nighter. It’s ten in the evening, and my computer simulation class starts at two the next afternoon. Sixteen whole hours. Finishing just one chapter every three hours will do nicely.

〰️〰️

Do those sixteen hours I’ve conjured up by staying awake all belong to me?

〰️〰️

And would every hour freed up by having no children be an hour I could spend concentrating? I doubt it. As I was saying, having a child might help me settle into something more deeply and do it well. Though if we’re talking about a newborn who wakes, sleeps, cries, and fusses at all hours and completely wrecks my sleep… well, that would be hard.

I like children, but I don’t consciously go around thinking, “I need a wife and kids.” Maybe a child is someone who comes along in the course of living, one more person to share the journey with.

If I did share my life with a child, I’d want to meet them as an equal. Another whole person living a life like mine and utterly different from it. That feels a little different from being what people call “a parent who’s like a friend.”

〰️〰️

Parent | Child | Lover | Friend | Teacher

Different, and much the same.
