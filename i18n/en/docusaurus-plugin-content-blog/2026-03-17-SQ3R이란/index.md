**SQ3R** is a way to study as you read.

The idea is to read deliberately, coming up with your own questions and looking for answers as you go.

## How I came across it

I used the sources below to put together an account of what SQ3R is and how to try it.

I first encountered it in [Growing Together, by Kim Chang-joon](https://product.kyobobook.co.kr/detail/S000001033071). Lately I’ve wanted to get better at picking up new things quickly and effectively, so I decided to look into it and give it a try.

<!-- truncate -->

## Survey

Hold off on reading the text itself. Look at the contents, titles, and subheadings to get a sense of the whole.

## Question

Write down questions about anything you couldn’t understand or anticipate during the survey.

For instance, what might a chapter called “The Programming Language Master” cover? Will it just tell me how good the expert is? Will they share how they got there? What would I have to do to get that good myself?

:::note
Reading with your own questions in mind gives you somewhere to direct your attention.
:::

## Read

Highlight or annotate the passages that bear on the questions you picked out. Give your eyes something to come back to.

## Recite

After reading the parts that address a question, try answering it out loud in your own words. With a bit of luck, your reading has given you what you need.

Make a note of any questions you struggle to answer. You’ll use them in the last step.

## Review

Go back through the questions you wrote down earlier.

Keep the questions physically separate from what you read. Quiz cards are one way to do it.

With the questions and the text side by side, you can pick up clues without noticing and mistake recognition for knowing the answer.

Give yourself the harder job of pulling it out of memory. After all, you generally can’t look it up during an exam! (Well, there are open-book exams…)
